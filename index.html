    The thirteen dashboard modules

    All thirteen modules are coded and share one mission state: your shelter, science stops, route and EVA window carry through the later modules and into the Mission Overview in Module 13.

    How to use this sandbox

    Move aroundDrag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Use ← and → to switch modules.
    Set your route endsChoose what a map click sets: destination, your position, or a point to inspect.
    Switch layersRelief, slope, hazard class and roughness can all be toggled and compared.
    Check the evidenceEvery module has an evidence table. Modelled values are labelled as modelled.

    Evidence

    NASA sources and data honesty

    Every number marked “NASA” on these pages comes from the pages below. Everything else is labelled as modelled or assumed.

    From NASA pages

    • MOLA datum and global range: −8,200 m to +21,300 m
    • Jezero: 45 km wide, ~18°N 77°E, lake about 3.5 billion years ago, floor about −2,600 m
    • HiRISE 30 cm/pixel, CTX 6 m/pixel, CRISM 18 m/pixel
    • Exploration Zone: ~100 km radius, up to +2 km, ±50° latitude
    • Landing-site coordinates from NASA PDS ODE
    • Landing hazards at Jezero: cliffs, dune fields, rocks
    • Jezero weather: −83 °C to −22 °C, 718 Pa, gusts about 10 m/s, at least 4 dust whirlwinds per sol
    • Radiation: 1.8 mSv/day in cruise, 0.67 mSv/day on the surface, about 1,000 mSv round trip, 250 mSv solar-event limit
    • Ice: Odyssey, SHARAD and HiRISE findings; MOXIE made 122 g of oxygen, peak 12 g/h
    • Comms, power, suit, robots and body: up to 22-minute one-way delay and 2° blackout threshold; dust opacity 1 to 5 and 30–40% storm light; 8 h and 10 h EVA design points and 350/530 W; Ingenuity and Perseverance speeds; bone loss of 1–1.5% per month and the 0.4 g warning
    • Astrobiology: Cheyava Falls potential biosignature in Bright Angel (Sapphire Canyon core), delta layering, Curiosity's Cumberland organics, and NASA's statement that proof needs Earth laboratories

    Modelled or assumed in this demo

    • The relief images are procedural and tuned to NASA's published figures. They are not MOLA or HiRISE tiles.
    • Elevations, slopes and roughness inside the Jezero region are modelled, with 167 m cells.
    • The delta, inlet valley, dune and boulder fields are placed approximately.
    • Slope bands of 15° and 25° and the difficulty score are team heuristics. Only the 5° value has a NASA source.
    • The walking pace is an editable assumption.
    • Module 3: the shape of the daily temperature curve and the dust-whirlwind timing curve are modelled around NASA's measured values. Suit limits are editable assumptions.
    • Module 4: walking-time map uses a hiking-speed formula from Earth and our own terrain model. Habitat shielding is an editable assumption.
    • Module 6: target scores are our rubric, our reading of NASA's text. Positions of the delta, Kodiak, Bright Angel and the outflow channel are schematic placements; only the Octavia E. Butler landing site is a NASA coordinate. NASA calls the Cheyava Falls finding a potential biosignature, not proof of life.
    • Module 7: routes are computed on our modelled terrain with our own cost weights; the recommendation score and the Go/Hold checks use team thresholds, not NASA operational limits.
    • Module 8: light time comes from simplified orbits; Mars time uses a Mars24-style formula (about 15 minutes accurate by our check). How long each decision can wait, and the Earth team’s thinking time, are our assumptions.
    • Module 9: NASA study values (33% cells, 12 h sun, 60% battery depth, 165 Wh/kg, opacity 1 to 5) are combined with our 50% array derate, 90% battery round trip and a straight line between NASA’s two opacity points. Sunlight at Earth (1,361 W/m²) is a standard figure not taken from the NASA pages we read.
    • Module 10: NASA’s 8-hour consumables table is scaled in a straight line by hours and effort. Water and food lines are ISS-era standards used as a planning reference. Contingency is our choice.
    • Module 11: robot counts, drone recharge time and scouting lead time are ours. Speeds come from NASA’s Ingenuity totals and Perseverance top speed.
    • Module 12: weight and fall figures are physics at 3.71 and 9.81 m/s². Countermeasure effect, rest sols and the 120 kg suit placeholder are ours. NASA says direct evidence for long stays at 0.38 g is not yet available.
    • Module 13: the Go/Hold verdict is a prototype advisory built from the thresholds above. Suit supply is a hard check; power, comms, scouting and body load are advisory rows.
    • Module 5: site scores come from our rubric, not from NASA. Site positions for Utopia Planitia and the equatorial region are approximate.
    • InSight's coordinates are rounded from memory and flagged on its card.
